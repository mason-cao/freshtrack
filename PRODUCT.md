# Product

## Register

product

## Users

FreshTrack is for people managing groceries at home: busy households, solo cooks, meal planners, and anyone who wants a fast read on what should be used next. They usually open the product while unpacking groceries, deciding what to cook, checking what is expiring, or reviewing whether food waste is improving over time.

Users are not doing deep inventory administration. They need quick recognition, light accountability, and low-friction actions: add an item, find what needs attention, mark it used or wasted, and get a recipe that helps prevent waste.

## Product Purpose

FreshTrack reduces household food waste by turning pantry inventory into an actionable freshness system. It tracks item freshness, highlights urgent food, suggests use-it-up recipes, records used versus wasted outcomes, and visualizes the value saved or lost.

Success means users can answer three questions quickly: what needs attention now, what can I cook with it, and am I wasting less over time?

## Brand Personality

FreshTrack should feel warm, practical, and encouraging. The tone is calm and useful rather than judgmental. It should make food waste feel manageable, not shameful.

The interface should feel like a well-kept kitchen notebook connected to a modern dashboard: fresh, tactile, organized, and quietly optimistic.

## Anti-references

- Guilt-heavy sustainability apps that make waste feel like failure.
- Clinical enterprise dashboards with cold grids, generic blue-gray palettes, and no food context.
- Diet or calorie-tracking aesthetics that turn food into punishment or optimization theater.
- Dark neon dashboards, purple gradient SaaS heroes, glassmorphism as decoration, and generic AI-generated card grids.
- Childish grocery apps that overuse emoji, oversized rounded shapes, or novelty illustrations at the expense of task clarity.

## Design Principles

1. **Action before inventory.** Freshness state should drive the screen. The product should always make the next useful action obvious.
2. **Warm accountability.** Show waste and savings honestly, but frame them as progress signals, not moral judgment.
3. **Food should feel tangible.** Use real food imagery, recognizable freshness language, and tactile surfaces where they help recognition.
4. **Density with breathing room.** The product can show useful data, but it should remain scannable on a phone in a kitchen.
5. **Delight only at outcome moments.** Motion and celebration belong to completed actions, state changes, and useful feedback, not decoration.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for contrast, focus visibility, keyboard navigation, and reduced motion. Color cannot be the only indicator of freshness status; labels, badges, and copy must carry the same information. Motion should respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, and primary inventory actions should be reachable without gestures.
